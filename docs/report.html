NBA Quant Lab Validation

Every player's minutes, points, rebounds, assists and the rest, forecast walk-forward from earlier games and the starting fives (who appeared stands in for the inactive list) and simulated possession by possession, scored against what actually happened. Tuned on 2023-24, then frozen and scored on 2024-25 and 2025-26.

2,460 test games52,947 test player-games 7,230 games reconstructed from play-by-play 63 µs per simulated game

What the evidence says

  1. Point forecasts: gradient boosting has the lowest MAE for 6 of 8 statistics, the others are close. Points MAE 4.376 (gradient boosting), 4.386 (team-first), 4.408 (simulation), 4.402 (closed-form empirical-Bayes rates), against 4.663 for season averages; on RMSE the team-first reconciliation is lowest for points (5.733 against 5.750). Most of the gain is the minutes model.
  2. Distributions: best calibrated for points, not better overall. The simulation's points PIT error is 0.034 against 0.122 for a mean-dependent negative binomial around the gradient-boosting forecast, but CRPS is a near tie for points and favours gradient boosting for PRA.
  3. Team forecasts: level with the team model. The shipped simulation's spread RMSE is 14.305 against 14.334 for the team model and its total RMSE 18.496 against 18.484; neither difference is significant. The bottom-up simulation alone is worse on both.
  4. Players must be modelled jointly. Summing independent player projections misses the team forecast by 5.9 points on average and implies a team-score SD of 18.7 against a realised 13.2. In the simulation every team score is the sum of its players' points.
  5. Honest gaps. At the line nearest the projection, over/under probabilities are overstated for rebounds, assists, threes, blocks by 1.8-3.5 points; the simulator plays overtime in 3.3% of games against 4.6%; who is available comes from who appeared, which flatters minutes by 0.066 (measured on 2023-24).

Over/under reliability

Reading it

Points on the diagonal mean a stated 60% over happens 60% of the time. Mean gap, predicted minus observed, at the line nearest the projection (percentage points): PTS +0.0, REB +3.5, AST +1.8, 3PM +1.9, PRA +0.6, STL -0.1, BLK +2.9, TOV -0.3; pooled over the ladder (the gaps differ by line; for rebounds, assists, threes and blocks the over-prediction shrinks at the lines above the projection): PTS +0.1, REB +2.7, AST +0.3, 3PM +0.7, PRA +0.7, STL -0.5, BLK +1.2, TOV -0.1. Every probability comes from the joint simulated games, so a two-leg parlay is the share of games in which both legs hit, never a product of marginals.

Matchups

Gradient boosting refitted twice, nested: once without the explicit opponent information (opponent style factors, the opponent factors inside the closed-form expectation, and the team-model environment, replaced by the team's own season-to-date averages) and once with it added back. Both refits' features differ from the shipped model's (no recent per-minute rates; the own-team averages are kept in both), so the with-opponent points MAE is 4.383 against 4.376 for the shipped model. Both refits also keep the projected minutes, whose blowout-risk features come from the team-model spread, so a little opponent information remains in the "without" refit. Gains are the reduction in MAE from adding the opponent information, with game-clustered bootstrap intervals.
