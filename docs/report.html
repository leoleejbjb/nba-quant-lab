Walk-forward backtest · NBA regular season

NBA Quant Lab Validation

Every player's minutes, points, rebounds, assists and the rest, forecast before tip-off and simulated possession by possession, scored against what actually happened. Tuned on 2023-24, then frozen and tested once on 2024-25 and 2025-26.

2,460 test games52,865 test player-games 7,230 games reconstructed from play-by-play 68 µs per simulated game

What the evidence says

  1. Point forecasts: simpler models are as good. Gradient boosting (points MAE 4.380), closed-form empirical-Bayes rates (4.406) and the possession simulation (4.412) sit within 0.7% of each other and 5-6% ahead of season averages (4.667). Most of the gain is the minutes model.
  2. Distributions: the simulation is the best calibrated. For points and PRA its PIT calibration error is 0.04 and 0.08 against 0.22 and 0.19 for negative-binomial distributions around the best point forecasts, and its 80% PRA intervals cover 79.7% against 73.8%.
  3. Team forecasts gain a little from the player layer. Alone, the bottom-up simulation is worse than the team model. Blended 25/75 with it, spread RMSE falls from 14.334 to 14.292 (squared-error reduction 95% CI +0.16 to +2.22); totals do not change.
  4. Players must be modelled jointly. Summing independent player projections misses the team forecast by 5.9 points on average and implies a team-score SD of 18.6 against a realised 13.2. In the simulation every team score is the sum of its players' points.
  5. Two mechanisms the data supports. Shot shares grow super-linearly with usage (alpha 1.3), and true shooting falls 0.88 points per +10% relative usage, estimated with teammate absences as a natural experiment. Opponent features help every statistic, by about 0.1%.

Over/under reliability

Reading it

Points on the diagonal mean a stated 60% over happens 60% of the time. Every probability comes from the joint simulated games, so a two-leg parlay is the share of games in which both legs hit, never a product of marginals.
